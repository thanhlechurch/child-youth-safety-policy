# Section 17: Illness, Allergies, Medication, and First Aid

## Section 17.1: Illness and injury

- Ministry-specific procedures shall identify exclusion symptoms, communicable-illness precautions, sanitation, and parent notification.

- All injuries requiring more than routine comfort or a simple bandage must be documented and communicated to the parent or guardian.

- Serious illness, significant injury, breathing difficulty, anaphylaxis, loss of consciousness, suspected head or neck injury, or other emergency requires immediate medical response and 911 when appropriate.

## Section 17.2: Allergies and medication

Medication and emergency-medication procedures must be reviewed by counsel, the insurer, and qualified medical personnel. TLC shall not adopt a blanket prohibition that prevents a child from receiving medically necessary emergency care.

- Parents must provide written medical and allergy information for designated events and programs.

- Medication must remain in the original labeled container and be stored securely, except when a child is authorized to self-carry emergency medication.

- Only a trained, authorized person may administer medication, consistent with the written plan and applicable law.

- Medication administration must be documented. When possible, two adults verify the medication, child, dose, route, and time.

- Emergency response takes priority over internal notification. Contact the parent as soon as practicable after emergency action begins.
