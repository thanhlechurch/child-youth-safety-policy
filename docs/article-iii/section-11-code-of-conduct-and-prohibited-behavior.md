# Section 11: Code of Conduct and Prohibited Behavior

## Section 11.1: Required conduct

- Treat minors with patience, respect, honesty, fairness, and age-appropriate care.

- Maintain observable, interruptible, and accountable interactions.

- Respect a minor’s stated physical and emotional boundaries.

- Use positive, constructive language and avoid humiliating or coercive spiritual practices.

- Report unsafe behavior, policy violations, grooming, abuse, retaliation, or supervision failures promptly.

- Cooperate with reasonable monitoring, documentation, and corrective action.

## Section 11.2: Prohibited conduct

- Sexual, romantic, flirtatious, exploitative, or dating conduct with a minor or current youth participant.

- Grooming a minor for a present or future relationship, including statements about dating after graduation or adulthood.

- Sexualized jokes, comments, images, media, games, conversations, or disclosure of a staff member’s or volunteer’s sexual or marital experiences.

- Private secrets, requests to delete messages, secret accounts, gifts intended to create loyalty, or isolating a minor from parents, peers, or other leaders.

- Favoritism that creates exclusive access, possessiveness, jealousy, emotional dependency, or repeated special rides, trips, or private meetings.

- Physical punishment, retaliation, degrading treatment, threats, shaming, hazing, intimidation, or using Scripture or authority to compel silence or affection.

- Being under the influence of alcohol, cannabis, illegal drugs, misused medication, or any impairing substance while responsible for minors.

- Possession of weapons or dangerous items contrary to TLC event rules or law.

- Entering private changing, shower, or sleeping areas contrary to policy; using cameras in areas where privacy is expected.

## Section 11.3: Adult responsibility, apparent consent, and improper defenses

Adults bear responsibility for maintaining appropriate boundaries with minors and current youth participants. A minor’s affection, initiation, apparent consent, maturity, participation, or failure to object does not transfer responsibility to the minor or make prohibited conduct acceptable.

None of the following excuses prohibited conduct, prevents a protective restriction, or requires TLC to restore ministry access:

- The adult claims good intentions, says the conduct was misunderstood, or denies romantic or sexual intent.

- No sexual contact has been proven, or civil authorities declined to investigate, charge, prosecute, or convict.

- The minor initiated, welcomed, reciprocated, or appeared comfortable with the conduct.

- The adult is married, the families are close, or the contact occurred within discipleship, counseling, ministry, or another trusted relationship.

- The adult is respected, gifted, needed, has served at TLC for many years, or has family and friends who support the adult.

- The adult apologized, expressed repentance, received forgiveness, or completed counseling or training.

- Enforcing a restriction would inconvenience a ministry, create staffing difficulty, affect preaching or leadership coverage, or cause embarrassment or division.

TLC evaluates objective conduct, patterns, access, power differences, impact, concealment, response to correction, and present ministry suitability—not merely a staff member’s or volunteer’s stated intentions.

## Section 11.4: Relational and emotional boundary misconduct

TLC treats relational and emotional boundary misconduct as a serious safeguarding concern. The conduct may be inappropriate and disqualifying even when no sexual act has occurred and the adult denies sexual or romantic intent.

- Repeatedly singling out the same youth for disproportionate attention, favors, gifts, privileges, rides, outings, or private time.

- Flirtatious, romanticized, possessive, jealous, or unusually intimate behavior, including unnecessary physical closeness or repeated touching.

- Becoming or seeking to become the youth’s primary or exclusive emotional confidant, or using the youth to meet the adult’s emotional needs.

- Creating private jokes, pet names, rituals, promises, secrets, or communication patterns that reduce appropriate adult and parental oversight.

- Competing with parents, peers, or other leaders for the youth’s loyalty, undermining those relationships, or presenting the adult as the person who uniquely understands the youth.

- Seeking repeated contact outside legitimate ministry purposes, including recurring private transportation, recreation, or informal gatherings.

- Continuing similar conduct after a concern, instruction, correction, warning, or restriction.

TLC does not need to establish sexual intent, physical or sexual contact, criminal conduct, or a completed act of abuse before imposing protective restrictions or determining that a person is unsuitable for youth leadership or access to minors. Relational and emotional concerns shall be evaluated cumulatively rather than as isolated incidents or explanations.
