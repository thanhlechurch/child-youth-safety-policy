# Section 16: Physical Contact, Discipline, and Behavioral Support

## Section 16.1: Physical contact

- Appropriate contact is public, brief, age-appropriate, responsive to the child rather than the staff member’s or volunteer’s emotional needs, and respectful of the child’s preference.

- Examples may include high-fives, fist bumps, handshakes, side hugs, or comforting contact appropriate to age and circumstances.

- No staff member or volunteer may force affection, tickle, wrestle, massage, sit a school-age child on the person’s lap, touch private areas, or engage in prolonged, secretive, romantic, angry, or possessive contact.

- A minor’s request not to be touched must be respected unless immediate safety requires intervention.

## Section 16.2: Discipline

- Use clear expectations, redirection, choices, calm correction, separation from the activity when necessary, and parent involvement.

- Physical punishment, humiliation, threats, food deprivation, forced exercise, isolation in a closed room, and spiritual shaming are prohibited.

- Physical restraint may be used only when no less restrictive option can prevent imminent harm, and only to the minimum extent necessary. The incident must be reported and documented immediately.

- Serious or repeated behavior may require a written support plan, parent meeting, adjusted participation, or removal when safety cannot otherwise be maintained.
