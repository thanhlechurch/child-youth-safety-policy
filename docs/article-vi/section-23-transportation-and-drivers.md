# Section 23: Transportation and Drivers

## Section 23.1: Driver approval

- Minimum age, license status, driving history, insurance, vehicle condition, and any insurer requirements must be verified before approval.

- Drivers must comply with seat-belt, car-seat, capacity, traffic, and mobile-device laws. Every occupant must use an appropriate restraint.

- No driver may operate a vehicle while impaired, dangerously fatigued, or using a handheld phone except as legally permitted in an emergency.

## Section 23.2: Accountability and group transportation

- Use two approved adults in a vehicle or one approved adult with multiple minors whenever reasonably practicable. One approved adult driver may transport multiple minors when the trip is authorized and the accountability requirements below are followed.

- Before travel, parents or guardians shall receive the transportation information appropriate to the activity. The responsible ministry leader shall document the approved driver, youth passengers, pickup or departure point, destination, expected schedule, and planned drop-off arrangement.

- Participating drivers shall use a TLC-approved ministry communication channel, such as an approved group chat, to report departure, significant delays or route changes, and completed drop-offs. The channel shall include the responsible ministry leader and shall be retained according to TLC’s records procedure.

- Drivers shall travel directly, avoid unauthorized or unnecessary stops, obey vehicle-capacity and restraint requirements, and promptly report any material change. A group chat or transportation log improves transparency but does not by itself authorize recurring private access to the same youth.

- Fifteen-passenger vans should not be used unless specifically approved by the insurer and operated under documented safety requirements.

## Section 23.3: One-adult/one-youth transportation and final-passenger safeguards

A planned one-adult/one-youth ride is an exception rather than the normal arrangement. Same-gender driver and passenger are preferred whenever reasonably available, but a cross-gender ride may occur when necessary if all required transparency safeguards are followed.

- The parent or guardian knows and approves the ride, and the responsible ministry leader knows the driver, passenger, purpose, pickup, destination, expected arrival, and drop-off plan.

- The trip is direct, with no unauthorized stops, and the driver communicates departure, significant changes, and completed drop-off through the approved ministry channel.

- When a route begins with multiple minors but naturally leaves one youth as the final passenger, the driver shall minimize the one-to-one portion, travel directly, avoid an extended private conversation in a parked vehicle, identify the beginning of the final leg when practicable, and confirm the completed drop-off.

- The same adult shall not routinely transport the same individual youth alone. A recurring arrangement requires documented approval by a Board-designated leader, parent involvement, a legitimate need, additional safeguards, and a defined review or end date.

- Convenience, recreation, friendship, longstanding custom, or a staffing shortage alone is not sufficient justification for recurring private transportation involving the same adult and youth.

## Section 23.4: Ministry-specific transportation procedures

The CM and Youth Ministry handbooks shall establish practical procedures for field trips, routine pickup and drop-off, driver and passenger assignments, approved communication channels, final-passenger safeguards, recurring-ride review, emergency transportation, documentation, and record retention. Event leaders shall plan transportation before departure rather than relying on informal arrangements after an activity begins.
