# Section 2: Biblical and Theological Foundation

TLC understands safeguarding as an expression of Christian discipleship, faithful shepherding, truthfulness, justice, repentance, and stewardship. The passages below are used according to the principles they teach in context. They do not replace civil law, professional safeguarding practice, or the specific procedures adopted in this policy.

> “Let the little children come to me. Don’t stop them...” (Mark 10:14, CSB)

In Mark 10:13-16, Jesus welcomes children when others attempt to hinder them. TLC therefore receives children as persons to be valued and cared for, never as inconveniences to be managed.

> “not lording it over those entrusted to you, but being examples to the flock.” (1 Peter 5:3, CSB)

In 1 Peter 5:1-4, church leaders are commanded to shepherd willingly and by example rather than domination. Ministry authority may never be used to obtain secrecy, personal access, emotional dependence, favoritism, or protection from accountability.

> “Speak up for those who have no voice, for the justice of all who are dispossessed.” (Proverbs 31:8, CSB)

Proverbs 31:8-9 calls those with authority to speak and judge righteously on behalf of vulnerable people. TLC will take concerns seriously, protect good-faith reporters, and avoid silence intended to protect status or reputation.

> “Therefore, putting away lying, speak the truth, each one to his neighbor, because we are members of one another.” (Ephesians 4:25, CSB)

Truthful documentation, reporting, and communication are obligations of life together in Christ. Concealment, retaliation, witness coaching, evidence destruction, and knowingly false statements are incompatible with this commitment.

> “I solemnly charge you before God and Christ Jesus and the elect angels to observe these things without prejudice, doing nothing out of favoritism.” (1 Timothy 5:21, CSB)

First Timothy 5:17-22 specifically addresses the treatment and discipline of elders. TLC reads the passage together: concerns involving leaders must not be handled carelessly, confirmed continuing sin must not be hidden, and decisions must be made without prejudice or favoritism.

> “The one who conceals his sins will not prosper, but whoever confesses and renounces them will find mercy.” (Proverbs 28:13, CSB)

Biblical repentance includes truthful confession and renunciation, not merely regret about consequences. Forgiveness, pastoral care, and restoration to fellowship do not automatically restore leadership, employment, or access to minors.

Matthew 18:15-17 is not a prerequisite to making a civil report or raising a safeguarding concern. A child, parent, reporter, or witness is not required to confront the person named in a concern. Scripture must never be used to delay reporting, compel secrecy, pressure forgiveness or reconciliation, or protect a leader from impartial review.
