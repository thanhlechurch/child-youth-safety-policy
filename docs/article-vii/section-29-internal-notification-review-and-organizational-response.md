# Section 29: Internal Notification, Review, and Organizational Response

## Section 29.1: Intake, immediate safety, and external reporting

TLC shall first address immediate danger, medical needs, missing-child concerns, and required civil reporting. Internal review, pastoral consultation, leadership consensus, prayer, or a desire to speak with the person named in the concern may not delay those actions.

After emergency and external reporting duties are addressed, a Safeguarding Lead shall open a confidential case record and notify the second Safeguarding Lead. When either lead is implicated or conflicted, the alternate route in Section 6 shall be used.

## Section 29.2: Temporary protective restrictions

- Separate the person named in the concern from minors and restrict access to facilities, rosters, electronic systems, transportation, counseling, discipleship, preaching, teaching, or leadership as the facts and risk require.

- Issue a written no-contact and non-retaliation direction when appropriate. The person may not contact the child, family, reporter, witnesses, or other participants about the matter except through the authorized process.

- Administrative leave or temporary restriction is protective and neutral. It is not a final finding of guilt and may remain in place while civil authorities, the church, or an independent reviewer completes appropriate work.

- **Written interim safety plan.** For a serious boundary, grooming, relational or emotional, retaliation, leadership, or abuse concern, TLC shall document any interim restrictions in a written safety plan. The plan shall identify the restrictions, persons responsible for enforcement and monitoring, permitted communication route, review date, consequences for violation, and any conditions imposed by civil authorities, counsel, or the insurer.

- **Direct and indirect no-contact.** Unless expressly authorized through the safeguarding process, a no-contact direction includes contact through a spouse, relative, friend, supporter, intermediary, group chat, social-media account, ministry gathering, or another person acting on behalf of the restricted person.

## Section 29.3: Documentation and preservation

TLC shall preserve messages, photographs, videos, attendance records, schedules, transportation information, access logs, prior complaints, training records, and other relevant information. Records shall distinguish firsthand observations from secondhand reports and shall identify the source of each material statement.

No person may delete, alter, hide, coach, coordinate, or retaliate regarding evidence or testimony. Such conduct is a separate serious violation.

## Section 29.4: Limited internal review

TLC does not investigate whether a crime or legally defined child abuse occurred before reporting. Internal review is limited to protecting participants, preserving information, evaluating policy compliance and ministry suitability, making employment or governance decisions, and cooperating with authorities.

The review may gather voluntary firsthand accounts, create a chronology, examine relevant records, and identify prior or related concerns. A child shall not be repeatedly questioned or subjected to leading, confrontational, or adversarial interviews. Interviews that could affect a civil investigation shall be coordinated with authorities and counsel.

## Section 29.5: Notice and opportunity to respond

After immediate safety, external reporting, and evidence-preservation needs are addressed, the person named in the concern shall ordinarily receive written notice of the conduct under review, the policy provisions involved, current restrictions, document-preservation duties, and the process for responding.

The person shall have a fair opportunity to provide a written statement or controlled interview to non-conflicted reviewers. The person may not confront or cross-examine a minor, obtain confidential records without authorization, demand private meetings with reporters, or use supporters to influence witnesses. Refusal to participate or dishonesty may be considered in ministry-suitability decisions but does not eliminate due process.

## Section 29.6: Findings

The authorized decision-makers shall make a documented, good-faith determination based on the reliable information available. Findings may include: no policy violation established; insufficient information to determine; operational or boundary violation; serious boundary, grooming, or leadership-misconduct violation; or abuse, exploitation, sexual misconduct, or other conduct requiring external action.

“Insufficient information” is not a finding that a report was false. A report shall be labeled intentionally false only when reliable evidence establishes deliberate fabrication, not merely because evidence is incomplete, memories differ, or authorities decline prosecution.

## Section 29.7: Written decision and communication

The final internal decision shall identify the findings, policy provisions, protective measures, discipline, duration and conditions of restrictions, restoration eligibility if any, appeal route, and required follow-up. Information shall be shared only with those who need it for protection, governance, care, legal compliance, or correction of a material misunderstanding.

## Section 29.8: Appeal or reconsideration

A person disciplined under this policy may submit a written appeal within 14 calendar days unless TLC governing documents require a different period. The appeal must identify a material procedural error, significant new information, conflict of interest, or clearly unsupported finding. Non-conflicted Board members or a Board-appointed outside reviewer shall decide the appeal. An appeal does not automatically suspend protective restrictions.

## Section 29.9: Independent review

TLC shall obtain independent assistance when a matter involves a senior leader, multiple possible victims, a repeat pattern across ministries, alleged institutional concealment, substantial conflicts of interest, significant public concern, prolonged unresolved delay, repeated missed response deadlines, loss of confidence in the internal process, or circumstances beyond TLC’s competence. The person named in the concern and close associates may not select or direct the reviewer. If a serious matter remains unresolved sixty (60) days after intake, non-conflicted Board members or their designees shall document the reason, confirm that interim safeguards remain adequate, and determine whether outside assistance is required. That review shall repeat at least every thirty (30) days while the matter remains open. Delay does not automatically lift restrictions.

## Section 29.10: Existing and historical concerns

Upon adoption, TLC may receive and review known or newly reported conduct that occurred before the effective date when the information bears on present ministry suitability, access to minors, leadership qualification, or risk to others.

TLC will not label conduct a violation solely because it failed to comply with a procedural rule that did not exist at the time. TLC may nevertheless consider whether the conduct violated then-existing law, prior instructions, biblical and ethical leadership duties, or commonly understood boundaries for adults serving minors.

Current protective restrictions may be imposed based on present risk even when the underlying conduct predates this policy. Prior incidents shall be considered cumulatively with later conduct when evaluating patterns, escalation, credibility, response to correction, and suitability.

## Section 29.11: Good-faith reporting, interference, and enabling

A good-faith safeguarding report made to a Designated Safeguarding Lead, another authorized TLC recipient, a parent or guardian when appropriate, or a civil authority is not gossip, slander, disloyalty, or divisiveness merely because it concerns a respected person or has not yet been substantiated. Knowingly false statements remain subject to correction and discipline, but an incomplete or unsubstantiated concern is not automatically false.

Good-faith questions about procedure, evidence, proportionality, or due process are permitted. They become improper when used to delay immediate protection or reporting, intimidate a reporter, influence witnesses, discredit a child or family, or preserve access for a person under restriction.

No person may knowingly help another person evade supervision, no-contact directions, communication limits, transportation limits, ministry restrictions, or facility-access rules. Prohibited enabling includes arranging informal access, allowing a restricted person to participate as an unofficial helper, facilitating private communication, withholding relevant prior concerns, or refusing to enforce an approved restriction.

Interference, retaliation, concealment, or knowing assistance in evading safeguards is a separate serious policy violation and may result in restriction or discipline even when the person was not involved in the original concern.

No person acting for, supporting, or closely associated with the person named in a concern may contact the affected minor or family to explain intentions, defend or minimize conduct, apologize, seek forgiveness or reconciliation, gather information, test whether the concern will be repeated, or influence how the concern is understood. Any necessary communication must be approved and coordinated by the Designated Safeguarding Leads. A violation of this requirement is interference and may result in separate restriction or discipline.

## Section 29.12: Case ownership, timelines, written plans, and process updates

Confidentiality protects children, families, witnesses, reporters, and a fair process; it does not authorize indefinite silence, unclear ownership, or an undocumented response. TLC shall provide process visibility without disclosing confidential testimony, private personnel information, or details that could endanger a child or interfere with civil authorities.

- **Acknowledgment and assignment - within two (2) business days.** A Designated Safeguarding Lead shall confirm receipt to the reporter and, when appropriate, the affected parent or guardian; identify the assigned case owner and child-and-family liaison; explain how additional information should be submitted; direct the preservation of relevant records; and remind the parties not to confront involved persons or discuss the matter in ways that could pressure witnesses.

- **Immediate safety and reporting assessment.** Emergency action and required civil reporting occur without delay. For a serious concern, an authorized, non-conflicted leader shall promptly document whether temporary restrictions, no-contact directions, parent notification, outside consultation, or other protective action are required. A decision not to impose an available safeguard shall state the reason and the date for reconsideration.

- **Written case plan - ordinarily within seven (7) calendar days.** The case owner shall document the issues under review, persons with decision authority, conflicts and recusals, immediate safeguards, records to preserve, appropriate information-gathering steps, outside-agency or professional involvement, communication recipients, next review date, and expected follow-up. The plan may be revised as facts or agency instructions change.

- **Periodic process updates - at least every thirty (30) days.** While a matter remains open, the assigned liaison shall provide the affected child or family and the reporter, as appropriate, a limited update confirming whether the matter remains active, whether relevant safeguards remain in place, whether action is required from them, and the date of the next update. Updates need not disclose confidential evidence, witness statements, employment information, or private discipline details.

- **Closure communication.** When the internal process concludes, TLC shall promptly tell the affected child or family and the reporter, to the extent appropriate and legally permitted, that the review has concluded; whether the concern was substantiated, not established, or unresolved; what safety-related restrictions or protections affecting them remain; how to report retaliation or new information; and whom to contact with future concerns.

- **Documented exceptions.** When law enforcement, child-protection authorities, counsel, the insurer, or immediate safety needs limit an acknowledgment, update, interview, or disclosure, the case record shall identify the limitation, source, date, and next review point. TLC shall provide whatever limited process information can be shared safely and lawfully.
