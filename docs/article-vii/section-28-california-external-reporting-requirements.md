# Section 28: California External Reporting Requirements

## Section 28.1: Mandated reporters

California law identifies, among others, administrators and employees of youth organizations and clergy members as mandated reporters in covered circumstances. Other roles may also qualify. Ordinary volunteers are generally not mandated reporters solely because they volunteer, but TLC may impose reporting obligations as church policy. Each person must know the duties applicable to the person’s role.

## Section 28.2: Timing and standard

A mandated reporter who knows or reasonably suspects that a child has been abused or neglected must make an initial telephone report immediately or as soon as practicably possible and submit the required written follow-up within 36 hours. Reasonable suspicion does not require certainty or proof.

## Section 28.3: Where to report

Reports may be made to a police department or sheriff’s department, excluding a school district police or security department; a designated county probation department; or the county welfare department. The current local contact list and California Suspected Child Abuse Report form shall be maintained in Appendix B and ministry response materials.

## Section 28.4: Individual duty and internal reporting

- The mandated reporter’s duty is individual. No supervisor, pastor, director, elder, or internal committee may impede, inhibit, approve, or substitute for the report.

- Reporting to TLC is not a substitute for reporting to an authorized agency.

- A team report is permitted only as allowed by law; a person who learns the designated reporter failed must make the report.

- TLC prohibits retaliation or sanction against a person who makes a report or raises a concern in good faith.

## Section 28.5: Persons who are not mandated reporters

TLC requires a staff member or volunteer who is not legally mandated to report known or reasonably suspected abuse promptly to an authorized agency or to participate immediately in a joint call with the Safeguarding Lead. The person always retains the right to report directly. Internal notification must occur after or contemporaneously with civil reporting and must not delay it.

## Section 28.6: Clergy communications

California’s clergy exception is limited to qualifying penitential communications. It does not remove a clergy member’s duty when acting in another mandated-reporter capacity. TLC will not broadly label ordinary counseling, discipleship, leadership conversations, or reports made for protection as penitential communications. Any privilege question must be handled promptly with qualified counsel and must not delay a report that is otherwise required.
