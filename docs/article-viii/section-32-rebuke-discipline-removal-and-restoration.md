# Section 32: Rebuke, Discipline, Removal, and Restoration

## Section 32.1: Biblical and policy purpose

TLC discipline seeks truth, protection, repentance, correction, justice, and the integrity of Christ’s church. It is not retaliation, reputation management, or a means of pressuring an affected person to reconcile.

> “Publicly rebuke those who sin, so that the rest will be afraid.” (1 Timothy 5:20, CSB)

First Timothy 5:19-21 addresses elders and must be read as a unit: accusations are not handled carelessly, confirmed continuing sin is not concealed, and discipline is administered without prejudice or favoritism. Public rebuke is not automatic for every mistake, and TLC will not disclose identifying or private details of a child or affected family merely to demonstrate that action was taken.

Galatians 6:1 calls spiritually mature believers to pursue restoration with gentleness while watching themselves. Gentleness does not require removing consequences, returning authority, or restoring access to minors.

## Section 32.2: Range of corrective and disciplinary action

- Coaching, clarification, retraining, written warning, increased monitoring, role modification, communication restriction, or a written corrective plan.

- Temporary suspension, administrative leave, no-contact direction, or removal from a specific assignment while information is reviewed.

- Removal from all minor-serving roles, leadership, preaching, teaching, discipleship, transportation, counseling, employment, office, or membership processes as appropriate under TLC governing documents.

- Reporting or disclosure to civil authorities, licensing bodies, insurers, employers, other churches, or organizations when legally required or permitted and reviewed appropriately.

## Section 32.3: Violation levels

- Minor operational lapse. An isolated, non-deliberate failure that created limited risk and is promptly acknowledged and corrected.

- Significant policy or boundary violation. Conduct that reduces visibility or accountability, creates unauthorized private access, disregards required procedures, or reflects poor judgment involving a minor.

- Serious safeguarding violation. Grooming behavior, secrecy, romantic or sexualized conduct, retaliation, dishonesty, evidence destruction, refusal to report, deliberate circumvention, exploitation of spiritual authority, or repeated significant violations.

- Abuse, exploitation, or criminal conduct. Conduct that may constitute physical or sexual abuse, neglect, trafficking, child sexual abuse material, exploitation, assault, or another offense requiring immediate external action.

TLC may impose stronger action than the examples above when the person’s position, access, intent, concealment, impact, prior history, refusal to accept correction, or risk to others warrants it.

## Section 32.4: Rebuke and formal discipline

A rebuke shall identify the conduct, the policy and biblical principles violated, the harm or risk created, the required correction, and the consequences of further violation. Vague requests to “be more careful” are not sufficient for a documented safeguarding violation.

Private corrective rebuke is ordinarily appropriate for a first lower-level violation that was not public and is honestly acknowledged. Broader or public correction may be appropriate when misconduct was public, involved a pastor or leader, continued after correction, materially damaged congregational trust, or was accompanied by misleading public statements. Any broader communication shall protect the privacy and dignity of children, families, reporters, and witnesses.

The appropriate non-conflicted governing authority shall impose discipline in writing. The person shall be told what conduct was found, what restrictions apply, how long they remain, what conditions must be met, whether restoration to any role is possible, and how to appeal.

## Section 32.5: Repeat offenses and patterns

TLC does not use a “three strikes” rule. A serious first offense may require immediate removal or permanent disqualification. At the same time, repeated lower-level conduct may demonstrate unwillingness to honor boundaries and may become a serious safeguarding violation.

- A same, similar, or related violation after instruction, training, correction, warning, or restriction is an aggravated violation.

- A pattern may be established by multiple incidents that surface at the same time, occurred before a prior warning, involved different minors, or occurred in different ministries, homes, trips, or communication platforms.

- Moving from Youth to CM, SALT, another congregation, an internship, a guest role, or an informal ministry setting does not reset prior restrictions or history.

- Failure to disclose a prior restriction, allegation, investigation, removal, or relevant disciplinary history is a separate violation and may independently disqualify a person.

- All substantiated violations, corrective actions, restrictions, and unresolved serious concerns shall be centrally recorded so that one ministry does not unknowingly treat a repeat matter as a first incident.

Relational and emotional boundary concerns shall not receive an indefinite series of informal conversations or vague reminders. TLC shall evaluate the full pattern, the adult’s position and access, the response to prior correction, and whether conduct is becoming more exclusive, romanticized, possessive, secretive, or dependency-producing.

- Depending on severity, an initial relational-boundary concern may require written correction, increased monitoring, and immediate limits on private communication, one-to-one transportation, counseling, mentoring, recreation, or off-site contact.

- A repeated or escalating concern ordinarily requires suspension from youth-facing duties, a cumulative review of prior concerns across ministries, and a written ministry-suitability decision.

- A romanticized, secretive, possessive, dependency-producing, or retaliatory pattern may require indefinite or permanent removal from youth ministry even when sexual contact or criminal conduct has not been established.

Ordinarily, a first minor lapse may result in documented coaching, retraining, and monitoring. A second similar or repeated violation should result in stronger action such as suspension, reassignment, a formal corrective plan, or loss of independent access. A third or continued violation, refusal to comply, or pattern showing escalating risk ordinarily results in removal from minor-serving roles and review of broader leadership or membership eligibility. These guideposts do not limit stronger action when safety requires it.

## Section 32.6: Repentance and restoration

Restoration begins with truthful acknowledgment and renunciation of wrongdoing, not merely regret about consequences. Evidence of repentance may include accepting findings without minimization, complying with restrictions, ceasing retaliation, correcting false statements, participating in appropriate counseling and accountability, making restitution where appropriate, and demonstrating sustained change over time.

The person who caused harm does not control the timing or form of an apology. A child, family, reporter, or witness shall not be required to meet, forgive publicly, reconcile, accept an apology, or support restoration. Pastoral care for the person disciplined shall be provided by people who are not simultaneously responsible for caring for the affected child or family.

Restoration to fellowship, where appropriate, is distinct from restoration to employment, office, preaching, teaching, counseling, discipleship, transportation, or access to minors. Ministry service is a trust rather than a right. Any possible restoration shall require a written plan, an extended period of demonstrated faithfulness, non-conflicted review, and a separate suitability decision.

## Section 32.7: Permanent or indefinite disqualification

Abuse, sexual exploitation, sexual or romantic pursuit of a minor, grooming, child sexual abuse material, trafficking, predatory conduct, deliberate concealment of serious abuse, retaliation against a child or reporter, or a repeated serious boundary pattern ordinarily results in permanent disqualification from child and youth ministry. Genuine repentance does not require TLC to restore access to minors or leadership authority.

A person permanently or indefinitely disqualified from ministry with minors may attend TLC only when permitted by law, counsel, and insurance requirements and under a written safety and participation plan approved by non-conflicted leadership. Such a plan may never place responsibility for supervision on a child, affected family, or ordinary volunteer.
