# Section 30: Pastoral Care, Parent Communication, and Public Statements

## Section 30.1: Care for affected persons

- Offer trauma-informed pastoral support without pressuring the child or family to disclose details, meet with the accused, forgive, reconcile, remain at TLC, or protect the church’s reputation.

- Use different, non-conflicted caregivers for the alleged victim or family and the accused person or family.

- Refer to qualified medical, mental-health, legal, advocacy, or victim-support resources when appropriate.

- Respect the family’s safety and privacy while complying with law and necessary organizational communication.

- **Designated child-and-family liaison.** For a serious concern, TLC shall appoint a non-conflicted liaison who is not simultaneously counseling, advocating for, or managing the response for the person named in the concern. The liaison explains the process in age-appropriate terms, communicates safety measures and permitted updates, receives reports of contact or retaliation, and helps connect the child or family with qualified support. The liaison does not conduct a forensic investigation or pressure the child to disclose additional details.

## Section 30.2: Parent notification

Parents or guardians should ordinarily be notified promptly of injuries, behavioral incidents, boundary concerns, or allegations affecting their child, unless law enforcement or child-protection authorities direct otherwise or notification may place the child in danger. The decision and basis must be documented.

## Section 30.3: Congregational and media communication

Only the Board-designated spokesperson may make public statements. Communications must be truthful, limited to verified and lawfully shareable information, respectful of privacy and due process, and must not minimize harm, attack reporters, speculate about motives, or claim exoneration before reliable findings exist.
