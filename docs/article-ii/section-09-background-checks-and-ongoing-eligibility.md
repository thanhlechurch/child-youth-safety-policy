# Section 9: Background Checks and Ongoing Eligibility

## Section 9.1: California baseline

Required by law, subject to counsel confirmation: California Business and Professions Code section 18975 requires administrators, employees, and “regular volunteers” of covered youth service organizations to complete child-abuse identification and reporting training and undergo the specified background check. A regular volunteer is an adult with direct contact with or supervision of children for more than 16 hours in a month or 32 hours in a year.

TLC will apply the required process to all current and new persons who fall within the law and to any additional role designated by the Board, insurer, or counsel. The requirement applies equally to long-time and new staff and volunteers.

## Section 9.2: Priority roles

- Pastors, administrators, employees, CM and Youth directors, core leaders, and safeguarding personnel.

- Regular classroom and nursery staff and volunteers, floaters, SALT disciplers, drivers, overnight leaders, and persons with keys or recurring access to minor ministry areas.

- Interns who will have recurring direct contact, contractors with repeated access, and any other person designated by counsel or insurer.

## Section 9.3: Financial access

TLC should budget for required screening and should not make personal financial means the default condition for service. TLC may use insurance or denominational discounts, group rates, designated gifts, reimbursement, or a phased board-approved rollout. Financial difficulty does not authorize service that violates law or policy.

## Section 9.4: Renewal and continuing disclosure

The background-check renewal interval and any subsequent-arrest notification process must be approved by counsel and the insurer. At minimum, staff and volunteers shall complete an annual renewal disclosure and immediately report arrests, restraining orders, child-welfare findings, misconduct allegations, or other developments that could affect suitability.

## Section 9.5: Disqualifying or restricting information

- A conviction, substantiated finding, admission, or credible evidence involving child abuse, sexual misconduct, exploitation, violence, trafficking, kidnapping, child pornography, or serious dishonesty related to the role.

- Placement on a sex-offender registry or a court, probation, parole, or child-welfare restriction involving minors.

- Refusal to complete screening, false statements, material omissions, or attempts to circumvent the process.

- A demonstrated pattern of grooming, retaliation, boundary violations, substance impairment, violence, or unsafe supervision.

A person may be restricted even when conduct is not criminal. TLC’s decision concerns ministry suitability and safety, not criminal guilt.
